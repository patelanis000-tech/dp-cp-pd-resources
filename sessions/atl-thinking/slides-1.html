Teacher Resource Highlight

Subject Reports

Why use subject reports?

  • See recurring strengths and weaknesses identified by examiners.
  • Notice patterns in misconceptions and exam technique.
  • Identify advice that can inform teaching and revision.
  • Compare several examination sessions rather than reading one report in isolation.
IB Subject Reports
3–5 years

Upload several reports together and ask AI to identify patterns across sessions.

Copy into NotebookLM
Review all of the uploaded IB subject reports for this course. Identify patterns that appear across multiple examination sessions. Focus on: - recurring student strengths - recurring student weaknesses or misconceptions - common assessment or exam technique problems - advice repeatedly given to teachers - topics or skills that examiners repeatedly say need improvement - any significant changes in examiner comments over time For each finding, state which report year(s) support it. Do not make recommendations that are not supported by the uploaded reports. Finish with a concise section titled “Implications for my teaching” containing 5 practical priorities based only on the evidence across the reports.

Approaches to Learning

The Five Approaches to Learning

ATL 1

Thinking

How students analyse, reflect, make judgments, generate ideas and become aware of their own thinking.

Critical thinking · creative thinking · reflection · metacognition
ATL 2

Communication

How students express, interpret and exchange ideas in different forms, situations and purposes.

Speaking · listening · reading · writing · presenting
ATL 3

Social

How students build relationships, collaborate, listen, negotiate and work effectively with others.

Collaboration · listening · negotiation · peer engagement
ATL 4

Self-management

How students organize their work and manage motivation, resilience, attention and state of mind.

Organization · time management · resilience · self-motivation
ATL 5

Research

How students find, select, evaluate, interpret and communicate information responsibly.

Questioning · searching · evaluating · synthesizing · referencing

IB presents the five ATL categories separately but emphasizes that they are interrelated and overlapping.
