High confidence

Secure

The student is correct and believes they can do it. Check whether the learning also transfers to a new question or context.

Misconception

The student is wrong but highly confident. This is important because the student may not realize that their understanding needs to change.

Low confidence

Developing confidence

The student may understand the skill but does not yet trust their own understanding. More independent practice may help.

Needs support

The student is unsure and incorrect. The knowledge, strategy or process is not yet secure.

Before checking answers

Ask for a confidence rating

Confidence alone is not enough. Correctness alone is not enough. Compare the two.

Example: “You said you were 4/4 confident, but the answer was incorrect. Let’s find the point where your thinking went wrong.”

Why this is useful

Students practise judging their own understanding against evidence. That makes metacognition more concrete and gives the teacher better formative information.

5/5 · How can we make thinking visible?

Explanations of reasoning

Ask students to show how they reached a conclusion, not only the final answer.

Confidence + correctness

Compare what students think they know with what they can actually demonstrate.

Reflection / self-assessment

Ask students to identify strategies, progress, challenges and next steps.

Transfer tasks

Use a changed context or unfamiliar problem to test whether learning can be applied independently.

Example: mind mapping before and after a topic

At the beginning, students record what they already know and the connections they can make. At the end, they return to the same map, add new learning, correct misconceptions and show new connections.
