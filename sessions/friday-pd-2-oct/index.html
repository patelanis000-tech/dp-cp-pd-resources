Mixed-subject groups12:20–12:30 · 10 minutes

A strategy that made a difference

Each person shares

One useful way you teach students to plan, organise or monitor their learning.

Use a real example you can explain in about a minute. Give everyone a turn.

  1. What did you do?Describe the routine, prompt or model.
  2. What did students do differently?Point to something you saw or heard.
  3. Where else could it work?Ask a colleague from another subject.
Spend the final two minutes comparing: which part of the strategy helped students take responsibility?
Group inquiry12:30–12:32 · 2 minutes

A common problem

Choose one that your group recognises.

  • 01

    Missed deadlines

    Students know the final date but struggle to plan the steps.

  • 02

    Repeated reminders

    Work moves forward when the teacher checks in.

  • 03

    Waiting for instructions

    Students hesitate to choose or begin the next step.

Describe one typical moment. What might the student need to learn or be able to access?
Group inquiry12:32–12:40 · 8 minutes

Support, responsibility & readiness

Useful support

What would you explicitly teach, model or make easier to access?

For example, model how to turn a deadline into smaller steps.

A gradual handover

Which decision or action could the student take next? What support would remain?

For example, the student proposes the next checkpoint.

Signs of readiness

What would you see or hear before reducing a prompt? What if the student still needs it?

For example, the student notices a delay and adjusts the plan.

Keep appropriate accommodations and routes to help. Agree one teaching response your group could explain.

Name one expectation jump. How could colleagues on both sides make the progression clearer?

Individual choice12:56–12:59 · 3 minutes

One small classroom trial

In my next suitable lesson, I will try …

I will look for students … as a sign that it is helping.

An example

Before a longer task, students propose their first two steps.

I look for whether they begin a suitable step without another prompt.

Choose a change small enough to try in normal teaching. Use work, talk or behaviour you already see.
